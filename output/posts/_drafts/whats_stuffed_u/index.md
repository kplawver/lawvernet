I love getting rid of stuff! It's my favorite hobby. Wedding dress? Going to be donated. Pretty knick-knacks and thingieamabobs that I never got around to putting up even though I had a plan? Donated. Toys that the kids haven't quite outgrown? Donated. I'm not sentimentally attached to much beyond photos and one or two childhood treasures. I do feel kind of bad when I get rid of a present I've received, but just decided to be thankful for the thought and clear out my closet anyway to make room for next year's presents.\\ It's no surprise then that my two favorite shows are **Designed To Sell** and **Clean House**, in which both shows basically tell people to get rid of their junk. This weekend I watched a two-hour special of **Clean House** where the show did the entire house (normally they do 3 or 4 rooms) for the winners of their "messiest house in America" contest. I was in heaven, yet appalled while watching. The winners of the contest had stuff stacked at least knee-high and, in many cases, waist-high, in every single room and the basement (3500 total square feet) so that they couldn't even see the carpet. It wasn't dirty with poop and maggots like that one Oprah episode, it was just cluttered.\\ Who needs 14 buckets of toys when your youngest is 24? GET RID OF THEM!! (Them being the toys, not the kids. The kids? Well, that is up to you.) A lot of the people featured on this show have extra clutter left from when their spouses or parents died and they can't bear to part with the stuff, even if it sits under a mountain of junk in the corner of the garage. (Holy run-on sentence, Batman!) One woman was like, "This is the juicer my grandmother once used." Oh, please. In this episode, the owner wanted to keep a _broken_ juke box because it was her late husband's. I'm like, "Take a damn picture of the thing and put it in the photo album next to his picture, but get rid of the jukebox anyway!"\\ Honestly, I am vaguely worried this will happen to me if my mom were to pass because she has some quirky collections. And SHE IS MY MOM and I LOVE HER, OMG. (Heh, that was me being dramatic. Did it work? Did you feel the drama?) I can picture myself cradling a spatula or a whisk and saying dramatically, "_This_ belonged to my mother," every time I used it. Which would probably only be once or twice a year since I don't cook. Somehow, my dad's John Deer tractor just doesn't hold the same allure.\\ The before and after pictures of the winners' house were amazing. Even more amazing though was all of the stuff they wouldn't part with. Two-thirds of their basement ended up being filled with large storage containers to hold this extra stuff. I'd estimate they had about 75 containers minimum. That was after they got rid of all of their junk and unneeded items, after they had experts and hosts and cameras stuck in their faces pleading with and bullying them into getting rid of stuff. Can you imagine? Suddenly, I am starting to.
