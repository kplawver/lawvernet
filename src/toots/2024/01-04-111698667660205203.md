<p>I'm spending a lot of time in the New York Times archives from the 1930s for a translation I'm working on, and it's very hard not to be distracted by the FABULOUS and often hiliarious ads.</p><p>"Why men stay home...LIEDERKRANZ CHEESE...and a beer in the icebox!"</p><p>"The tiny fur hat—a tremendous success"</p><p>"Patum Peperium, the Gentlemen's Relish, is English, and very spicy served on toast or crackers, as canapes. 3-oz crock, 59¢. The crock itself is also ensnaring."</p><p>"Pepless...irritable...yet nothing really wrong. It's ASTHENIA (Run-down condition, usually caused by Constipation). Take Pluto Water every morning."</p><p>"Afraid of coffee? Then here is good news!"</p><p>"Imagine! A *round* bridge cloth - McCutcheon's newest deal for the bridge hostess"</p><p>"A NEW Hair Law, by Charles Nessler, Inventor of Permament Waving, Author of the book Story of Hair, etc. Write for free booklet Errors and Illusions in the Hair Field"</p><p>"What! SHIRTWAISTS AND SKIRTS FOR EVENING? Yes! Shirtwaists and skirts for evening!"</p><p>"A Truly Unusual Offering of 417 Pairs of Draperies of Superior Types"</p><p>Along with endless ads for fur coats, cigarettes, and alcohol to be sold after the repeal of Prohibition at the end of 1933. It's wild.</p>
