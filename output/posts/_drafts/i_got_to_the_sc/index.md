I wrote this up for posterity- heh- as this is the closest thing I have to a journal.\\ I got to the school to see Brian holding an ice pack on his arm. He'd literally just come down the slide. He was frowning and said his arm hurt. I figured he would shake it off and be well again in a few minutes. He was treating his arm carefully, like a wounded puppy would. He wanted me to carry him, so I lifted him like a bride and off we went to the car. By the time we got there, a whole three minutes later, I knew something was really wrong. I called my MIL on my cell phone to ask whether I should take him to the ER or an urgent care. She said she'd call to see what facilities have an x-ray machine. I called her back a few minutes later and she found one just a few miles away. She offered to go with me and have my FIL watch Max. I dropped Max off at her house and picked her up and continued on to the mini-ER. The drive from the school to her house and then the ER was really sucky. Brian alternated between crying and whimpering. At one point, Max and I sang "Old MacDonald" to distract Brian from the pain. He was in a booster seat, but slumping over to the side. Every turn and bump hurt him. I was relieved when we finally could get him out of the car.\\ Once at the ER, we only had to wait about 15 minutes before being seen. The dr ordered morphine and X-rays for Brian. We were there from 6 until 12, waiting, waiting, waiting for the dr to see us, for X-rays to be taken, for the dr to come back to us, for the orthopedist to look at the X-rays. In the end, they determined Brian did have a broken arm and sent us home with pain meds, his arm roughly wrapped and instructions to call the orthopedist on Monday.\\ Brian spent the weekend on the couch, hopped up on pain medicine and surrounded by ice packs, with me by his side. He was still in a lot of pain and would wake up and cry or whimper every 20 minutes or so. By Monday or Tuesday, he was ready to stop merely watching tv and wanted to play with toys. Toys! One night he wanted to play soccer but he was really wobbly on his feet. Kevin literally held him up, like you would an older baby, and we kicked the ball back and forth. Brian was so giggly and high. The night before and the day leading up to surgery, he wasn't allowed any pain meds, yet he acted the same as usual. This leads me to think that I was over-medicating him, even though I was well within the safety guidelines.\\ We were able to see an orthopedist on Tuesday and got Brian into surgery on Wednesday. While in the prep room, Brian and Kevin played with a homemade bear given by the hospital. They named the bear Tootie because he had the power to fly when he farted. Brian was so calm and well-behaved in the prep room. The nurse came over to him and explained that he would take a nap, the dr would fix his arm, and then he would wake up to see Mommy and Daddy. Then she said, "Ready for your nap?" Brian closed his eyes and she wheeled him away. She said, "You can open your eyes, we haven't arrived at the OR yet." He just shook his head no and refused. It was cute but he was probably scared. Once in the OR, the doctor just needed to set the bone and cast his arm. We went home about 2 hours later.\\ The next day Brian seemed a million times better and we mostly resumed normal life. The couch was forever ruined, but it had died a few months (years?) before so it was no great loss. The first few days after getting the cast, Brian would hold his own hand, probably trying to distribute the weight some. He would sit gorilla-like, with his arm perpendicular to the floor. Brian adapted quickly to eating and drawing with his left hand.\\ Two weeks after the accident, we sent him back to school. He wasn't allowed to participate in gym time or play on the playground, but everything else went smoothly. The teachers would take markers and paper out for him and would draw with him. He had only been attending the school for two weeks when the accident happened, and then he was out for two weeks, so starting again was a lot like starting over. He was sad and nervous to be dropped off. Within a few minutes though, he would be involved with an activity and be fine.\\ Thus endeth the whole story.
